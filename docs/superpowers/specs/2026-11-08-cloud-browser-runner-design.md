## What exists (read 8 Nov 2026)
- **The container** (`jobadabra-web:ops/hosted/ready-fill/main.mjs`): `POST /run {site, token}` starts a night; `GET /state`, `WS /ws` (the live view), `GET /handoff`, `POST /close`. Every call needs the night token; nothing is written to disk but the browser's temp profile.
- **The Worker** `jobadabra-web:workers/ready-runner` starts it per account; the image is checked by `ready-fill-image.yml` before each deploy.
- **The shared night files** (this repo, `desktop/ready-fill/`): `night.mjs` (one browser, the night's copy of the extension, the stand-in app, one tab per job, counts only to the Worker, the cost cap), `fill-job.mjs` (one job), `no-submit.mjs` (the net that refuses a submit request), `live.mjs` (live view, hand-off).
- **Cost** (`night.mjs` costMicro, measured): the container ≈ $0.000012 a second (standard-1), so a 30 s form read ≈ $0.0004 a job, plus the AI the extension reports.

## Design (states, their one owner, the verdicts)
- **One runner, one task per run.** `POST /run {site, token, task}` with `task` one of `readiness | fill | auto` (missing = `fill`, so today's nights are unchanged). The night code picks the per-job step by task; browser start, extension copy, stand-in, tabs, live view, cost cap and `/close` stay shared.
- **What each task may do:**

  | Task | Started by | Reads the form | Fills | Presses Submit | Result back |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | `readiness` | Always on with no Mac (Prepare top matches, a scheduled run) | yes | no | never | the Apply readiness read per job (the same shape as `lib/apply-readiness.js` stores) |
  | `fill` | Ready to send (today) | yes | yes, held for the person | never | held tabs, hand-off (today) |
  | `auto` | Full automation with Always on (later) | yes | yes | only through the one gate below | the job's outcome and the page's own proof |

- **Where the result lands:** a `readiness` read goes to the job's `apply_readiness` field through the store the person chose (Always on + Notion: the Applications row). With the default store (this Mac) and the Mac off, the site keeps it encrypted with the account's hosted state (where Ready to send's cloud state already lives), and it is removed on the site once the app has read it (owner, 8 Nov 2026: "Site holds it, encrypted (Recommended)").
- **The app's seam:** `lib/apply-readiness.js` `readForm(job)` is the one place a read happens; R2 (PR "[ac] Every Apply goes through Apply readiness") routes every caller through it. The cloud runner becomes a second `readForm` used only when Always on runs without the Mac.

## Safety (CLAUDE.md "Safety" applied to a new place; for the owner's yes before any Submit code)
1. Submit from the cloud only in task `auto`, only with the item's own stamped confirmation (`autoConfirmedAt` carried by the queued item, as on the Mac), never from a saved setting alone.
2. The page's own proof always holds (every required field filled, a sending button, no bot check); kit eligibility holds unless the person turned on "Apply to jobs marked not eligible".
3. `readiness` and `fill` keep `no-submit.mjs` as the net; `auto` presses Submit through one gate function with its own guard test.
4. Never a login wall, a captcha, an SMS code or a disguised browser (honest user agent, no stealth plugin); a 401/403/429 ends that job, no retry.
5. Tests, the image check and the pool never submit: the image check runs `readiness` and `fill` against the stand-in site only.

## auto: Full automation from the cloud (cs, 10 Nov 2026)
The owner's decision (10 Nov 2026, asked via AskUserQuestion with the sketch https://claude.ai/artifact/Fv3pEUbwmzw4qML9fv7s8V, relayed by the coordinator): "Build it now (Recommended)": a Pro account on Always on gets its jobs submitted by the cloud browser while its Mac is off; "~500/day + alert (Recommended)": an abuse ceiling only, about 500 cloud submits per install a day, the owner told once when one is hit. No normal daily limit (owner, 29 Oct 2026: "remove all checks"). The 5 safety lines above stay as written.

- **States and their one owner.**

  | Where | States | Its one owner | What changes them |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | The Mac's queued item (`Background Apply` section) | queued (mode `auto`, `autoConfirmedAt`, `chosenAt`, `allowIneligible`, `session`, `queuedAt`) → `cloud` (sent) → receipt_confirmed / a hand-off | `lib/background-apply-store.js` | the person's pick (queue), sending to the site, the outcome the app takes back |
  | The site's job row (`ready_jobs`) | queued → filling → `reserved` (its one attempt) → `submitted` / `not_submitted` (reason) / `unknown` (pressed, no receipt) | jobadabra-web `api/ready.js` | `/reserve` and `/result`, with the night token only |
  | The press | refused (reason) or one permit | `desktop/ready-fill/submit-gate.js` | its inputs below |
- **The gate's inputs** (`submitVerdict`, then `gateSubmit`): task = `auto`; the item's own stamp (mode `auto`, `chosenAt`, `autoConfirmedAt` a time not in the future and no later than `queuedAt`; the item's job, address and session present and equal to the job being pressed); the item still live (state `queued` or `cloud`, no `manualOverride`; the site's Stop for that job); within 24 h of when the site received the batch (`sentAt`, the site's clock); the last HTTP status of the page (401/403/429 ends the job); not already attempted; the page's own proof and kit eligibility through `lib/submit-proof.js` (one definition with the Mac's `reserve`); the judge's explicit `botCheck: false`; `readyAt` (when the judge said ready); the item's own wait (`waitMin`: an item without one is refused, never pressed on a default). Every refusal but `waiting` is final: the job goes back to the person, never tried again. A pass asks the site to reserve the job's one attempt before the press; a refused or failed reservation never presses.
- **What the Mac adds when it sends (cs 4/4):** the store item as it is (`session`, `state`, `manualOverride`, `mode`, `chosenAt`, `autoConfirmedAt`, `allowIneligible`, `queuedAt`), plus the job's id and address and Settings' wait (`config.auto.waitMin`) read at send time, plus the job's stage and kit eligibility. These are a snapshot: the Mac reads the wait, the stage and the kit at press time; with the Mac off nothing re-judges them, so a change the person makes on the Mac later (a re-judge, Applied, Stop, Cancel all) sends a Stop for that job to the site (`/api/ready/stop`, the route a readiness night uses), and the gate refuses a stopped job.
- **Where the stamp travels:** the Mac's store item → the site with its job (`/batch` task `auto`, each job with its own item; a job without one is refused, and the site never makes or copies a stamp from a setting) → sealed in the batch → the container's gate. Settings are never sent and never read by the gate.
- **The ceiling:** `/reserve` takes one of the install's ~500 cloud submits of the UTC day in one statement after the job's one attempt, keyed by the queued item (install, the Mac's session, job) in `ready_attempts`, which no night, Stop or sweep deletes, so a re-sent item is never pressed twice (the same shape as the readiness ceiling, `ready-read-access.js` `reserveOwn`); the first refusal of a day tells the owner once (switchboard event `ready-submit-ceiling`); the job is `not_submitted` (ceiling). Who may start `auto`: a Pro account on Always on (`isPro`), its container seconds charged to its credit like a read; `READY_AUTO = off` closes it.
- **What comes back:** per job `{job, task: auto, state: submitted | not_submitted | unknown, why, proof: {signature, submitControl, requiredComplete, botCheck}, attempt, ms, costMicro}`; the site seals it like a read, keeps it up to 30 days until the app takes it (a press the Mac never hears of is the worst case) and removes it once taken (`/reads` returns them as `submits`, with `pendingSubmits` still running). The app writes it into the item (receipt_confirmed, or the Mac's own hand-off reasons: `submission_outcome_unknown`, `not_eligible`, ...) and lists the run in Recent activity.
- **Tests never submit:** the gate is tested as a pure function with a fake reservation; the night's `auto` is tested against the stand-in catcher only; the image check keeps running `readiness` and `fill` (line 5).
